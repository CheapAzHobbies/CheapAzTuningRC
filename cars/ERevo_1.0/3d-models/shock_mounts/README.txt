Summary:
Custom Shock Mounts for True 1/8 Big Bore Shocks on Revo Gen 1. Works with Swaybars with slight trimming. Provided Editable Step files if you want to mod the files and STL files for 3d printing.These custom mounts let you fit actual 1/8 scale big bore shocks (like HotBodies/HPI) onto the Revo Gen 1. My setup is tuned for racing—not for sending it to the moon—but feel free to take it as far as you want.How to Set Up HPI/HotBodies Shocks (Front and Rear):Drill the stock eyelet: Enlarge the hole in the stock shock shaft eyelet until it reaches the ball joint.Trim the eyelet: Shorten the eyelet so that the threaded part of the shock shaft can screw in deep enough to just touch (or nearly touch) the ball.Assemble internals: Install the original bump-stop inside the shock body. Then stack the kit washer then 1.2mm, 4-hole piston ton top, fasten the piston with the provided lock nut. Fill with 80wt oil—this is your baseline for both front and rear.Cap setup: Press-fit the ball joint from the original GTR cap or eyelet into the new shock cap. Make sure it sits flush.Note: 50wt oil in the stock Revo Gen 1 shocks feels similar to 80–90wt in these HB/HPI big bores.For springs, use Axcess Springs—the same ones listed in the comparison chart I provided. I bought mine from www.thespringstore.com. You can reference the chart to mix and match. Personally, I run Gold in the front and Tan in the rear. As a general rule: go softer in front, stiffer in the rear. For example, if you want Silver up front, you’d pair it with Blue in the rear, following the chart’s order. BIG BIG ISSUE IF YOU USE THE SPRINGS I RECCOMENDAlso I wouldn't use the 60mm springs provided in the picture, they will wear and you'll get near perfect ride height over time and have the correct amount of droop, but you should just got 50-55mm springs. I'm pretty sure anything shorter than 55mm is perfect, it would almost cover the whole shock body an look super clean.
