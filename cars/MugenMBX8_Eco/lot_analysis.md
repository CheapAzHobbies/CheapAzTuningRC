## The big catch: MBX7 ≠ MBX8 (and truggy ≠ buggy)

Two filters decide whether a spare is worth anything **to you**:

1. **Generation.** Mugen's **MBX8 (2017) was a clean-sheet redesign** over MBX7/MBX7R/MGT — diffs, gearboxes, uprights, arm mounts, and driveshafts changed. Mugen prints the fitment on every bag, so the tag is ground truth:
   - Tag lists **MBX8** (e.g. `MBX8/7R`, `MBX8/7/MGT`) → **fits.** ✅
   - Tag lists **only MBX7 / MBX7R / MGT / MBX6** → older gen, **won't fit the MBX8.** ♻️ resale
   - Generic `MBX/MGT` on **diff internals** = the **MBX7-era diff**, *not* the MBX8 diff → treat as MBX7.
2. **Body style.** Your car is a **buggy**. The two store-repackaged bags are labeled **`MBX8Te`** = MBX8 **Truggy** electric. Truggy arms are longer/wider than buggy arms, so the **`RC57532` arm kit won't fit your buggy**; the `RC55509` turnbuckle kit *might* (lengths differ) — verify.
3. **Power.** Electric car → the nitro **`E2712` engine-mount washer** is useless.

The upshot: this looks like a seller who ran an **MBX7/MGT** car for years (hence the deep MBX7 spares hoard) and the MBX8 buggy is a newer/separate addition. Most of the hoard belongs to that older car, not yours.
