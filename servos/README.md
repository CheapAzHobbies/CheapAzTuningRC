# Servo Value Matching — Premium vs. Cheap Alternative

> **Generic reference, no single winner.** The goal isn't picking one best servo, it's finding a **cheap servo whose real torque and speed at the voltage you actually run match a quality servo's**, so you aren't paying brand premium for performance you're not using. **Not tied to any one car** (shared, like [`batteries/`](../batteries/) and [`controllers/`](../controllers/)). **Headline finding: the $19.65 PTK 9752TG-D matches the $129.99 ProTek 160TBL on both torque and speed at 7.4V**, and beats it on torque by 5%.

## Why match per voltage, not headline spec

A servo's box usually prints **one** torque number, tested at whatever voltage makes it look best, usually the top of its range (2S LiPo, 8.4V, HV). That number alone doesn't tell you what the servo does at the voltage your setup actually feeds it.

Torque and speed both climb with voltage on the same servo. So a $130 quality servo's printed "20.9 kg·cm" doesn't mean a $20 servo claiming "25 kg·cm" is better, if the cheap one's number was measured at 8.4V and the expensive one's at 7.4V. Line them up at the same voltage or the comparison is meaningless.

**The matching method here:**
1. Take the quality servo's torque and speed **at every voltage its spec sheet lists** (typically 4.8V, 6.0V, 7.4V, 8.4V).
2. Find a cheaper servo that clears the **same numbers at the same voltage** you'll actually run, not just at some higher voltage.
3. Check what torque and speed matching doesn't cover: **stall current**, **signal refresh rate**, **dead band**, **gear material**, **spline count**, **case sealing**. A torque-matched cheap servo is still a bad swap if it needs more current than your BEC supplies, or if its spline won't take your servo saver.

Step 3 is where most of the real dealbreakers live, and it's the step the cheap brands document worst. See [BEC sizing and current draw](#bec-sizing-and-current-draw).

## How much torque you actually need

Buying torque you don't use costs money, weight, height and current draw, and usually costs speed too. These numbers come from what this build group has actually run, not from a marketing chart.

| Class | Torque at 7.4V | Evidence from this group |
|---|---|---|
| **1/10 4x4 short course / buggy** | **20–25 kg·cm** | The PTK's 22 kg·cm drives an [aluminum bell crank](../cars/FastAzJato4x4/steering_bell_crank_analysis.md) at 4S without stalling. The JX CLS6322HV's 21 kg·cm was the standard for years across 16 units |
| **1/10 monster truck** | **20–25 kg·cm** | The [E-Revo 1.0](../cars/ERevo_1.0/README.md) runs two PTKs at 22 kg·cm each |
| **1/8 buggy / truggy** | **35 kg·cm and up** | The JX CLS6336HV's 35.6 kg·cm is validated on Mike's Arrma Kraton. A 22 kg·cm PTK **died** on Jose's Team Associated ET48 truggy (2026-09-07) |
| **X-Maxx / heavy crawler / 1/5** | **45–70 kg·cm** | Outside this group's cars. This is Savox SB-2290SG territory and the only tier where the premium price tag buys torque nothing cheap matches |

**The 1/8 line is the one that matters.** Everything at or below 1/10 monster truck is served by a $20 servo. Above it, you need roughly 60% more torque, and that's the point where the cheap options thin out.

## Spec format

> *Every row's Spec cell uses this order, `N/A` where unknown:* **Type · Performance · Gears · Case · Motor · Bearing · Spline · Refresh · Pulse width · Dead band · Neutral · Travel · Signal · Programmable · Stall current · Waterproof · Size · Weight · Price**
>
> This replaces the standard `Torque · Speed · Voltage` fields (registered in [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) for single-voltage servo docs) with one combined **Performance** field, since the whole point here is comparing torque *and* speed together at each voltage. It also adds **Spline**, **Stall current** and **Waterproof**, the three cross-checks that decide whether a torque-matched swap actually works.

**Performance is a curve, not one number.** For every voltage the spec sheet lists, low to high, one `<br>`-separated line, torque and speed together:

```
**Performance:** 4.8V: 15kg·cm @ 0.095s/60°<br>6.0V: 18kg·cm @ 0.072s/60°<br>7.4V: 22kg·cm @ 0.060s/60°<br>8.4V: 25kg·cm @ 0.052s/60° (peak)
```

**Use whatever voltages the servo's own spec sheet actually lists.** Don't invent a 4.8V or 6.0V figure a manufacturer never published just to fill the row. Most sheets cover **4.8V and 6.0V** (what a basic analog BEC feeds) plus **7.4V and 8.4V** (2S LiPo / HV), but not every servo publishes all four. Torque printed in oz-in is converted at **13.8875 oz-in per kg·cm**.

## Spline compatibility

Torque and speed matching means nothing if the horn or servo saver won't go on. Spline count is set by brand family, not by price tier, and the three standards do not interchange.

| Spline | Brands | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **25T** | Futaba, Savöx, Traxxas, ProTek, Reedy, AGFRC, Hitec (H25T on the D-series), and most Chinese servos including JX and PTK | **The de facto standard for RC cars.** If you're swapping between anything in the tables above, your existing horns almost certainly fit |
| **24T** | Hitec (older and non-H25T models) | Check the individual model. Hitec uses both, so brand alone doesn't tell you |
| **23T** | Sanwa (formerly Airtronics), KO Propo, JR | Won't mesh with a 25T horn. Forcing it strips the horn |

**Two traps.** First, **rebadged servos carry the spline of whoever actually made them**, not the brand on the case, so check the servo rather than the label. Second, a **25T-estimated** entry in the tables above (the PTK and the SB-2273SG) means no manufacturer figure was published and Servo Database inferred it. Confirm before ordering an expensive aluminum horn.

## BEC sizing and current draw

**This is the cross-check that decides whether a torque-matched swap actually works**, and it's the field the industry documents worst. Two of the fifteen servos in this doc publish a stall current figure.

| Servo | Idle | No load | Stall | BEC needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Savöx SB-2290SG** | N/A | 180mA @ 6.0V · 220mA @ 7.4V · 220mA @ 8.4V | **7.5A @ 6.0V · 9.2A @ 7.4V · 10.5A @ 8.4V** | **>10A recommended by Savöx** |
| **Hitec D954SW** | 30mA | 500mA | **5.2A** | ~6A |
| *Everything else in this doc* | — | — | **not published** | Infer from torque class |

**What the two published figures tell you about the rest.** A 29 kg·cm coreless servo stalls at 5.2A. A 70 kg·cm brushless servo stalls at 10.5A. Torque and stall current climb together, so a 35 to 40 kg·cm servo (the JX CLS6336HV, the AGFRC) should be budgeted at roughly **6 to 8A**, and a 20 to 25 kg·cm servo (the PTK, the ProTek 160TBL) at roughly **4 to 5A**. Those are inferences from two data points, not published numbers, so treat them as a floor for sizing and not as spec.

**This group's field evidence lines up with that.** The PTK browned out a Castle Mamba X's **3A** internal BEC at full lock on the E-Revo 1.0. The same servo runs fine on the Hobbywing MAX8 G2S's **6A/15A** BEC, and two of them run fine on it. The rule this repo uses: **verify the ESC's BEC is ≥5A before running a PTK-class servo, and add an external BEC if it isn't.**

## Notes

- **Match at the voltage you'll actually run, not the servo's best-case voltage.** A cheap servo that only hits the premium servo's torque at 8.4V is not a match for a build feeding it 6V.
- **The premium tag buys three things, and torque is only one of them.** Brushless motors (longer life, less heat), sealing, and dealer support. Below 40 kg·cm none of those are worth 5× the price on a basher. Above it, the Savöx SB-2290SG genuinely has no cheap equivalent.
- **Documentation quality does not track price.** The $87 AGFRC publishes a four-voltage curve and an IP67 rating. The $159.99 ProTek 170TBL won't publish a bare servo weight or its dimensions. The $130 ProTek 160TBL doesn't publish stall current, and neither does the $20 PTK.
- **Dynamic torque and static holding torque are different numbers.** The ProTek 170SBL publishes both, and its static figure is **26% below** its dynamic one. Every other servo in this doc quotes a single number, and it is almost certainly the dynamic one, so cross-brand torque comparisons carry that much slop by default.
- **Brand honesty on spec sheets is still an open question for this doc.** Every figure above is the spec sheet's claim except where a build in this group measured it (noted per row). Flag known-inflated brands and models here as they're identified.
- **Torque and speed matching is necessary but not sufficient.** Cross-check stall current, signal refresh rate (analog 50Hz vs digital 300Hz+), dead band, spline, and case sealing separately.
- Full ownership history, failure modes and BEC gotchas for the PTK and JX servos live in [`FastAzJato4x4/servo_analysis.md`](../cars/FastAzJato4x4/servo_analysis.md); price history in [`Deals/servos.md`](../Deals/servos.md).
